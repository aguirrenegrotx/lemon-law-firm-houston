New Car Lemon Law in Houston, TX

We review new-car repair records for repeat defects across the Houston area.

Call (346) 214-1590 Ask a question

A returning warning light, a transmission noise, or a stall after several shop visits is alarming in a new vehicle. The repair order may say the technician could not duplicate the issue, even though the same symptom returns on the road. We review records for owners in Houston, West University Place, Southside Place, and Bellaire.

Repeated visits can create a trail of repair orders, mileage readings, and dealer notes. New Car Lemon Law matters often turn on that trail, not just the warning light or noise itself. We compare the reported fault with the work listed on each shop invoice.

This is worth looking at now rather than setting aside. The sequence of repair visits, warranty reports, and correspondence is easier to establish while repair orders and dealer records can still be located.

Warning lights and repeat repairs

Owners often describe an engine light that returns after a reset, a screen that freezes or goes black, brakes that vibrate after service, or a transmission that jerks during acceleration. Other calls involve a vehicle that will not start, a battery that repeatedly drains, a fuel odor, or a roof that leaks after rain.

A repair order may use different wording than the owner uses on the phone. We look for the same concern across each visit, including related complaints such as loss of power, rough shifting, or repeated electrical failures. These situations can sound similar from the driveway, but the repair history and written diagnosis can point to very different record questions.

Case review and repair orders

We begin a case review with the vehicle's main problem, the known repair visits, and whether the vehicle was bought or leased. The first conversation usually settles the basic timeline and the symptoms reported to the shop. Exact dates, mileage, and repair descriptions need the paperwork in front of us.

We gather the purchase or lease agreement, repair orders, dates in and out of the shop, warranty documents, and correspondence with the dealer. We then place the repair visits in order and compare the reported fault with the technician notes and completed work. We also check whether the same system, such as the transmission, electrical wiring, or engine, appears across multiple records.

Repair attempts, warranty dates, downtime

We look closely at the number of repair attempts, the days the vehicle was out of service, and whether each visit produced a written repair order. A vehicle may have several shop visits, but the records need to show what was reported, what the technician found, and what work was performed or declined.

Warranty dates also matter. We check whether the fault was reported while warranty coverage was running and whether later records connect back to an earlier complaint. The purchase or lease agreement, the mileage at each visit, and the person holding the dealer records can all affect how clearly the repair history can be assessed.

Repair orders and VIN

Repair orders should show the complaint, the date the vehicle entered and left the shop, and the mileage reading. The purchase or lease agreement usually identifies the vehicle, the seller, and the transaction date.

The VIN can be found on the dashboard plate, registration, insurance card, or purchase paperwork. We also review dealer emails, text messages, and appointment confirmations that mention a warning light, noise, or return visit.

Keeping repair orders in date order helps us compare each complaint with the work performed. Call Lemon Law Firm Houston at (346) 214-1590 with the records nearby; the connection between a reported fault, its warranty date, and each repair visit gets harder to reconstruct as documents scatter.

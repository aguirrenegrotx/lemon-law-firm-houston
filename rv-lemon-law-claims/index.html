RV Lemon Law Claims in Houston, TX

We review RV repair records, warranty documents, and recurring defects in Houston.

Call (346) 214-1590 Ask a question

Most RV owners take the coach back to the dealer first. That is the sensible response to a warning light, leaking roof, or slide-out that will not retract.

A repair order can show what the technician saw, what work was attempted, and whether the same fault returned. Repeated shop visits can leave an owner with a stack of papers but no clear account of the defect.

This is worth looking at now if the RV has returned for the same concern or stayed in the shop. Repair dates, mileage, and warranty status are easier to evaluate while the service history can still be assembled.

Warning lights and slide-outs

A check-engine or chassis warning light that returns after a repair is a common concern. Owners also report a slide-out that binds, stops halfway, or will not retract before travel.

Other calls involve water entering around a roof seam or window after rain. A generator that will not start, shuts down under load, or loses power can create its own series of shop visits.

These complaints can sound alike from a driveway, but the component, repair history, and written diagnosis may require a different review.

Case file review

Our case file review begins with the purchase or lease agreement, repair orders, and warranty booklet. We read the complaint line, technician notes, diagnosis, parts listed, and mileage on each service record.

We then place every repair order in sequence using the dates the RV entered and left the shop. We gather correspondence with the dealer, including emails, text messages, appointment confirmations, and service updates.

The first conversation usually identifies the present defect, the available records, and whether the RV was purchased or leased. The paperwork is needed to verify repair dates, shop time, warranty reporting, and whether one concern appears under changing descriptions.

Repair orders, shop days, warranty

The number of repair attempts matters, especially where repair orders describe the same symptom or component each time. We also look at the days the RV was unavailable for shop work, including the date it went in and the date it came out.

A written record matters. A technician notation, invoice, email, or text message may show that the concern was reported and what response followed.

We check whether the issue was reported while the written warranty was active, whether the RV was bought or leased, and who holds the service records. Missing papers do not end a review, but they can limit what the repair timeline establishes.

Repair orders and VIN

Find the purchase or lease agreement and warranty booklet before calling. They are often in the delivery folder, lender packet, or email from the sale.

Set aside every repair order, even one marked unable to duplicate. The VIN, mileage, date in, date out, and complaint line help us place each visit on one timeline.

Keep dealer emails, text messages, and appointment confirmations with those records. Those messages can show reports made before a scheduled repair visit.

Auto Dealer Fraud in Houston, TX

We review sales papers, dealer disclosures, and repair records for fraud concerns.

Call (346) 214-1590 Ask a question

Most owners return to the dealership after a surprise appears in the vehicle, payment papers, or sales paperwork. That is a sensible first step because the service desk or sales manager may correct a recording error or explain a document.

That approach can stop working when the explanation changes, a promised repair is never written on a repair order, or the signed agreement does not match the discussion. A verbal conversation rarely resolves a conflict over a VIN, mileage disclosure, trade-in figure, or add-on.

This is worth examining now, not setting aside. Sales papers, text messages, and the vehicle’s condition at delivery are easier to trace before later repairs and transactions blur the record.

Payment changes and missing options

People call us saying the monthly payment or financed amount is higher than the figure discussed at the desk. Others find an add-on product, service contract, or equipment charge in the purchase agreement that they did not recognize at signing.

Another complaint is prior collision damage, water damage, an odometer issue, or a missing feature discovered after delivery. Some owners say a warning light returned after the dealer described the vehicle as repaired or inspected.

Those reports can sound similar from the driveway, but the sales form, disclosure, inspection note, and repair order can point to very different questions.

Case file review

We use the first conversation to identify the vehicle, the dealer transaction, and the central mismatch. We can usually establish whether the concern involves a purchase or lease, a condition issue, a financing paper, or a promised repair.

Next, we read the purchase or lease agreement, financing papers, repair orders, and dates the vehicle entered and left the shop. We gather correspondence with the dealer, advertisements, disclosure forms, inspection sheets, and photographs where available.

Then we compare the VIN, mileage, charges, vehicle description, and written statements across those records. Questions about what was said, what was signed, and whether a fault was documented need the paperwork in front of us.

Repair attempts, warranty, and service days

The number of repair attempts matters when the reported condition is part of the dispute. We look at each repair order, the complaint recorded by the shop, the diagnosis, and whether the same warning light, noise, or failed component returned.

Days out of service also matter. Dates in and out of the shop show whether the vehicle was unavailable, but only if the orders and appointment records support them.

Warranty status can affect how we organize a file, especially if the fault was reported before coverage ended. We also check whether the vehicle was bought or leased and who retains the sales and service records.

A signed repair order and a dated message often give us more to check than a recollection without a document. Missing papers do not end the inquiry, but they affect what we can compare.

Repair orders and VIN

Keep the purchase or lease agreement and every repair order near the phone. The agreement usually lists the VIN, sales date, vehicle description, charges, and signatures.

Find the delivery checklist, window sticker, advertisement, financing forms, and any text or email discussing condition, options, payment, or repairs. Repair orders should show the mileage, complaint, dates in and out, and work performed.

Auto Dealer Fraud in Houston, TX

We review sales records, repair orders, and dealer representations.

Call (346) 214-1590 Ask a question

Many people call us saying, “They told me the car was fine,” or “They said that problem was already fixed.” Those statements can point to an auto dealer fraud concern when the sales paperwork, repair history, or vehicle condition tells a different story.

Other callers say the warning light returned on the drive home, the mileage did not match what they expected, or the dealer added charges they did not understand at signing. We translate those driveway complaints into records we can review.

This is worth looking at now because the sale documents, repair orders, text messages, and vehicle condition are easier to match to the transaction before the paper trail becomes scattered.

The warning light returned

We hear that the check-engine light came back after the dealer said the vehicle was repaired. We also hear about a transmission noise that was called normal, an air conditioner that stopped cooling, or a vehicle that went back to the shop more than once for the same complaint.

Some owners report that a prior accident, water damage, mileage issue, warranty problem, or earlier repair was not explained before the purchase or lease agreement was signed. Others say a promised repair, feature, or service contract does not appear in the final documents.

These complaints can sound similar from the driveway, but the repair order, sales statement, odometer reading, and correspondence may lead to very different questions.

Case file review

We begin a case file review by hearing the sequence of the sale, the first vehicle problem, and each contact with the dealer. The first conversation usually settles the basic transaction date, the vehicle involved, and the statement or repair that raised concern.

We then gather the purchase or lease agreement, buyer's order, repair orders, dates the vehicle entered and left the shop, warranty papers, and correspondence with the dealer. We compare the written descriptions against the mileage, dates, and concerns reported at each visit.

We can identify the broad record trail during an initial conversation. The exact sales representations, repair history, and document language need the paperwork in front of us.

Repair attempts, warranty, records

We look at the number of repair attempts for the same fault and the days the vehicle was out of service. A repair order matters most when it identifies the complaint, the technician's findings, the work performed, and the mileage at the visit.

We also check whether the fault was reported while the warranty was running. A warning light, brake concern, engine noise, or electrical failure may appear differently in a repair record than it was described at the service counter.

The purchase or lease agreement can affect which documents apply to the transaction. We also consider who holds the repair orders, the dealer messages, the vehicle history report, and any document describing the vehicle's condition before the sale.

Repair orders and VIN

Before calling, we ask owners to locate the repair orders and the VIN. The VIN is usually visible on the dashboard near the windshield, on registration papers, and in the purchase or lease agreement.

We ask for each shop date, the mileage shown on the repair order, and the complaint written by the service department. The purchase documents may show an addendum, vehicle history report, warranty form, or listed equipment.

We also review texts, emails, advertisements, and dealer messages that describe a repair, condition, or promised feature. Call Lemon Law Firm Houston at (346) 214-1590; reconstructing the vehicle's condition and the dealer's disclosures at the sale can become harder with time.
